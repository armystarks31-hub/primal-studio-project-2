Photography with a personal perspective

Photography gives me a way to notice what makes a person or moment distinctive. Expression, posture, light, and the setting all affect how an image feels. My approach begins with paying attention to those details and considering how they work together. The studies above explore how a simple background and directional light bring attention to a person’s expression and style.

Portrait photography connects naturally with my background in barbering. Both involve looking closely at individual style and understanding how people want to present themselves. A portrait can document a new look, support a personal brand, or simply mark a particular moment in someone’s life. The goal is to make that purpose visible through the image.

Planning a session

When you contact me, tell me what the photographs will be used for and the feeling you want them to have. You can share ideas about clothing, locations, or examples that help explain your direction. Graduation photographs might celebrate an achievement, while a portrait session may focus on professional presence or a more relaxed personal image.

For event photography, include the occasion, general location, and the moments that matter most to you. We can discuss the scope, timing, and availability directly.

Behind the photograph

I start by choosing how much of the setting belongs in the frame. A close portrait highlights expression and grooming. A wider frame can show the setting and make room for movement. For an outdoor graduation portrait, I’d look for open shade and use the campus as part of the composition.
